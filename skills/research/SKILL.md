---
name: research
description: Investigate questions and compare products or options using current sources, relevant criteria and cited evidence.
---
Establish what decision or answer the user needs. Use their budget, region,
constraints and preferences from the conversation. Infer harmless defaults;
ask for a missing constraint only when it changes the recommendation materially.

Use web_search for discovery and web_fetch for primary pages. Use the browser
for interactive pages, current checkout details, account-specific information
or JavaScript content. Follow promising links and read beyond search snippets.
Compare like-for-like prices, availability and specifications. Include material
tradeoffs and evidence against your preferred option. Mark missing information
and distinguish measured facts, claims and your judgment. Cite the pages that
support the recommendation. Keep conclusions proportional to the evidence.

For broad research, delegate independent questions with a shared comparison
rubric and expected source URLs. Reconcile contradictions before recommending.
Keep source dumps in worker context and durable artifacts; bring useful findings
back to the user. Stop when the requested decision is supported, rather than
collecting sources indefinitely. Make a purchase only within the user's actual
authorization, after checking the final item, price and account.
